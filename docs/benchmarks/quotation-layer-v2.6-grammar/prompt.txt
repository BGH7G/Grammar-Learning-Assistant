Input kind/role/ctype are UNVERIFIED hints from a previous pass, not facts. Analyze the actual tokens in context. Never invent omitted subjects merely to justify a hint. If a hint conflicts with the words, describe the mismatch briefly in notes and analyze the actual direct structure. Do not invent new enum values or output IDs.
Reporting/mental verbs: when say/think/believe/claim etc. is followed by a complete subject+predicate content clause, keep that WHOLE clause as ONE object node, kind=clause, ctype=noun, expandable=1, even with omitted that or partial quotation marks. Its subject belongs INSIDE that object, never alone as the reporting verb's object. Do not apply this rule to a mere noun object or a separate independent quoted sentence.
Coordination: decide what is coordinated, not from comma or quotation marks. One subject with two finite verb groups normally has ONE predicate phrase containing coordinate predicate phrases; these are NOT coordinate clauses. Expand that predicate into phrase/coordinate/none siblings. Independent clauses with their own subjects are clause/coordinate/coordinate siblings. Commas may coordinate predicates. Nonfinite clauses and imperatives may lack explicit subjects; do not invent subjects or change their word ranges.
A sentence-initial participial adjunct such as Referencing ... has role=adverbial, ctype=participial; its understood actor normally comes from the main subject. Discourse markers such as sentence-initial Well use marker with a Chinese note, never adverbial merely because they introduce the sentence.
Punctuation does not create clause boundaries by itself. Prefer attaching opening quotation marks to the following content and closing marks to the preceding content within contiguous sibling spans.
