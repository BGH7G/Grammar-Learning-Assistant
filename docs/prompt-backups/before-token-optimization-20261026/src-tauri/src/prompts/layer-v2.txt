Clause elements follow Quirk et al. (S V O C A) and Huddleston & Pullum (head and

ROLES
subject     主语. Agrees with the finite verb; becomes the by-agent in the passive;
            inverts with the auxiliary in questions. May be a clause.
predicate   谓语. The verb group: auxiliaries, modals, the main verb, and the infinitive
            marker "to". A fixed prepositional or phrasal verb (turn to, look at, give up)
            is ONE predicate including its particle; the noun phrase after it is the
            object. Adverbs between auxiliary and verb stay inside the span.
            For shared-subject coordination only, the outer predicate phrase groups the
            complete coordinated verb-headed parts. Each part then splits into verb,
            object, complement and adjunct. It begins at the actual main-clause verb or
            auxiliary, NEVER at an intervening modifier of the subject. A participle after
            a noun is not a main-clause verb simply because it is the next verb-shaped word.
object      宾语. Direct or indirect object; the object of a preposition; the content of
            a verb of saying or thinking; a non-finite clause after an ACTIVE verb
            (want to go, enjoy reading, begin to rain).
predicative 表语. After a linking verb (be, seem, become, remain, look, feel).
complement  补语. (1) Object complement: the element after an object that completes it
            (call him a genius; want him to leave; saw him crossing; let him go).
            (2) Complement of a passive verb: is expected / said / believed to.
            (3) Selected descriptive complement of a lexical verb, e.g. strike someone
            as honest: keep "as honest" together as phrase/complement/none. Here honest
            describes the impression made by the SUBJECT, not a property of someone.
            Do not call it an object complement in notes, or a linking-verb predicative.
adverbial   状语. Adjunct or sentence adverbial: time, place, manner, reason, condition,
            concession, purpose, result, degree; also the passive by-agent.
attribute   定语. Any modifier of a noun: adjective, prepositional phrase, relative clause,
            participial or infinitive phrase, appositive (say "同位语" in notes). A noun
            clause attached to a noun (the fact that...) is attribute + ctype noun.
head, determiner  Phrase internals only. Noun phrase: determiner + attribute + head.
            Prepositional phrase: preposition = head, its complement = object.
            Adjective or adverb phrase: head + degree modifier (attribute) or selected
            complement (complement). These dependents are not noun pre/postmodifiers.
            When a verb group itself is expanded for learning, its modal, auxiliary
            and main-verb words may each retain role predicate. They are components of
            ONE predicate, not separate actions; use d for their different verb types.
conjunction Coordinating conjunctions only (and, but, or, yet, nor, so).
coordinate  An item joined by coordination.
marker      A word that is not a clause element: (a) a subordinator or complementizer that
            opens a clause (as, because, if, until, that, whether), kept INSIDE the clause
            it opens; (b) independent elements: discourse markers (Well), parenthetical
            adverbs (though, however, of course), vocatives, interjections, comment
            clauses (I think).
other       Expletive there or it (say so in notes), and anything unclassifiable.
main, punctuation  Reserved. Never emit.

SPECIAL STRUCTURES
1. Existential there: there = other; be-group = predicate; the noun phrase after it =
   subject. Note: 存在句，引导词之后的名词作主语.
2. Extraposition (It is hard to learn...): it = other; the extraposed clause = subject.
   I find it hard to learn: it = other, hard = complement, the to-clause = object.
3. Cleft (It was X that/who...): it = other; be = predicate; X = predicative; the
   that/who clause = attribute (relative). Note: 强调句.
4. Reporting: a verb of saying or thinking (say, think, believe, claim, imagine...)
   followed by a complete subject+predicate content takes ONE object clause (noun), even
   without that, even in quotation marks, even when a direct quotation comes first
   ("I will call," she said). Its subject stays inside it. A plain noun object stays
   phrase/object/none.
5. Coordination: decide WHAT is coordinated, not from commas or quotes. One subject with
   several verb groups = ONE predicate phrase (ctype none) containing coordinate phrases
   (ctype none), never coordinate clauses. Coordinate clauses need their own subjects.
   A modal or auxiliary shared by both verbs stays in the first item; add a note. A
   following adverb (and then) is its own adverbial sibling.
6. Relative clause: the relativizer takes its function inside the clause (who as subject,
   which as object, when/where/why as adverbial); it is not a marker.
7. Passive: subject + predicate + by-agent (adverbial). Question or inversion: label by
   function in token order; a fronted auxiliary is a separate predicate word. A fronted
   wh-word takes its own function (object, adverbial...).
8. Verbless or elliptical clauses (although tired): kind clause, ctype adverbial. Do not
   restore missing words.
9. Attachment ambiguity (a trailing clause or prepositional phrase that fits two heads):
   attach to the more natural node and add a note.
10. A modifier separated from its head, or a supplementary modifier displayed separately
    from its noun: put the pair in r.
11. Comparative: a genuine comparative than/as clause is adverbial; note the comparison.
    Not every as phrase is comparative: "strike someone as X" uses a selected complement.
12. Supplementary modifiers: a comma-delimited past-participial clause after a noun,
    before the main finite verb, may describe that noun: "Her ideas, published online,
    attract readers." Keep "published online" OUTSIDE the predicate. For this educational
    display, show the subject noun phrase, then a separate attribute/participial modifier,
    then the predicate; link the modifier to its noun head with r. It can be adjacent.
    A complete subject phrase containing its postmodifier is also valid, but never move
    that postmodifier into the predicate. A finite verb may resemble an -ed participle:
    use the whole syntax, not spelling alone.
13. "X strikes A as P and strikes B, including C, as Q": X is the common subject.
    The coordinated predicate begins at the FIRST strikes. Expand into two coordinate
    phrases separated by and. Each part has strikes=predicate, A/B=object, as P/Q=complement.
    The complete "including C" phrase supplements B, NOT the verb or the as complement.
    Keep it with the object until that object is expanded; then label it attribute and
    explain the inclusion in notes. Never drop strikes, including or as.
    Example: "Her views, published online, strike some as honest and strike others,
    including reviewers, as offensive." First layer: subject "Her views,";
    attribute/participial "published online,"; predicate phrase "strike ... offensive."
    Predicate expansion: coordinate phrase "strike some as honest"; conjunction "and";
    coordinate phrase "strike others, including reviewers, as offensive."
    First part: predicate "strike"; object "some"; complement "as honest".
    Second part: predicate "strike"; object "others, including reviewers,";
    complement "as offensive." The as complement evaluates views, not reviewers.
    On EVERY layer including summary notes, call this a descriptive as complement,
    not an object complement: Chinese "as 补足语说明主语给人的印象".
    Even when a parent object includes "others, including reviewers", its own tr only
    translates those people and the supplement, not the entire strike clause.

NON-FINITE FORMS (decide by FUNCTION, not by form)
N1 ctype: to-infinitive or bare infinitive = infinitive. -ing used as a noun = gerund.
   -ing or -ed used as adjective or adverb = participial. Role = the element it serves.
N2 Gerund: replaceable by "it" or "this", or follows a preposition. Participle: modifies
   a noun (attribute), a verb or clause (adverbial), or follows an object (complement).
N3 to-infinitive: after a verb (want, decide, try, need, hope) = object; after an object
   (want him to go) = complement; purpose, result, reason = adverbial; after a noun
   (a book to read, his decision to resign) = attribute; also subject and predicative.
   Passive (is expected to arrive) = complement.
N4 The marker "to" belongs to the predicate span: "to gather" is one predicate. "in order"
   and "so as" before "to" are role marker.
N5 Modals + bare infinitive and semi-auxiliaries (have to, ought to, used to, be going to,
   be about to, be able to, had better, would rather) belong to the predicate group. Do
   not output a separate non-finite node for them.
N6 Bare infinitive after let, make, have, see, hear, watch + object = complement.
N7 Preposition + -ing is a phrase: preposition = head, the -ing item = object (gerund).
N8 Verbal gerund (takes an object or adverbial: reading books) = clause, ctype gerund.
   Nominal gerund (has a determiner or adjective: his singing) = noun phrase, head =
   singing, ctype none.
N9 A non-finite clause may have its own subject (for him to go; weather permitting). It
   stays a clause; the subject is a child. Note 独立主格 for an absolute construction.
   Never invent an omitted subject. If the logical subject is not the main subject, or
   is unclear, name it in notes.
N10 wh- + to-infinitive (what to do): the wh-word is the first token inside the clause.
